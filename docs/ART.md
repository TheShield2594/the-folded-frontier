# Hand-made art

All of The Folded Frontier's art is drawn in code, so the game ships with no image files apart from the title screen. That works well for anything that repeats, varies or changes with settings. It works less well for a few large or constantly seen pictures, where a hand-painted image would look better than shapes built from `rr`/`poly`/`circ`. This page lists which art is worth replacing and explains how to add a picture.

## How it works

Put a PNG or WebP in `assets/art/` and it replaces the drawn art of the same name. Anything without a file keeps its procedural art, so the art can be swapped one picture at a time and the game always runs.

```
assets/art/atlas/<name>.png    one 64×64 atlas cell, named after its key in C (atlas.js)
assets/art/sheets/<name>.png   one whole sprite sheet, named after its key in SHEETS
assets/art/rigs/<rig>.<part>.png  one part of a paper cutout rig (rig.js): slime.body.png, human.head.happy.png
```

- **Atlas cells** (`C.swFe` → `assets/art/atlas/swFe.png`). Cells that are arrays take the index after a dot: `C.crack[1]` → `crack.1.png`. A cell covers the block, decor or item icon everywhere it is used: the world, the backpack, tooltips and the held item.
- **Sprite sheets** (`SHEETS.stag` → `assets/art/sheets/stag.png`) hold the animation frames side by side (the mount's 3 frames of 160×112 make a 480×112 strip). The new image must have the same size and frame layout as the drawn one. Other sizes are scaled with a console warning.
- **Rig parts** (`assets/art/rigs/`): every character, foe, partner and boss is a paper cutout rig whose parts are drawn in code and baked into one skin (`rigSkin` in `rig.js`). A picture named `<rig>.<part>` replaces that part in every skin of the rig; a variant (an expression, a blink) adds its name, as in `human.head.happy.png`. Rigs that several foes or townsfolk wear with different colors take the skin key after an `@`: `slime@slime.body.png` is only the green slime's body, `slime@bslime.body.png` the blue one's, and `human@guide.head.png` only the Guide's head. The picture is stretched over the bounds the drawn part covers, so its pivot and joints stay put: paint it at the drawn part's proportions (2× its size is plenty), without the cream edge, which the rig adds to every piece. Parts not painted (the slime's eyes and mouth, which blink and move) stay drawn on top. New rig art re-bakes the skins, and the still pictures made from rigs (foe and partner sheets, townsfolk portraits, card faces, bestiary sketches) are redrawn. A merged piece joins parts into one painting: `<rig>[@key].<part>+<part>...` covers the first part and the ones after the `+`, which stop drawing, so a body can be painted with its wings or arms on (`bat@bat.body+wingA+wingB.png`); `<part>+all` joins every other part, and `root+all` makes a whole cut-out that moves as one piece from its feet (`human@knight.root+all.png`). A merged piece keeps its painting's proportions: it is as tall as the drawn parts it covers, centred on them (cut it with `rig_parts(..., keep=True)`). The player's own look is built from colored layers, so the human rig without a key is best left drawn until it has painted layers of its own.
- The files are found at build time (`import.meta.glob` in `src/art.js`, through the `@art` alias in `vite.config.js`) and bundled by Vite, so a missing picture never makes a request or logs an error. Images under 4 kB are inlined into the bundle, and larger ones get a hashed file in `dist/assets/`.
- At boot `loadArt()` decodes the images and paints them over the drawn cells and sheets. It then rebuilds the atlas normal map (so the world lighting uses the new art), clears the cached icons and bestiary sketches, and marks the sheet textures for upload. `artReady` is a promise that resolves to the number of pictures applied.
- Ore cells (`copper`, `iron`, `gold`, `frostOre`, `inkOre`, `emberOre`, `foilOre`) are only replaced while Settings > Color vision is off. In the other modes the drawn ores come back, because their nugget shapes and palette carry the meaning (`applyCB()` repaints the overrides after it redraws the ores).
- Names that don't match a cell or sheet are skipped with a console warning.
- Everything in `assets/art/` ships with the game, so never put test pictures there. The smoke tests glob their own from `tests/fixtures/art/` (`FF_ART_DIR`), and CI checks that none of them reach `dist/`.

### Making a picture

1. Run `npm run dev` and open the dev console.
2. `g=await import('/src/game.js')`, then `g.artList()` lists every replaceable name with its size (rig parts under `rigs`).
3. `g.artExport('sheets','stag')`, `g.artExport('atlas','swFe')` or `g.artExport('rigs','slime.body')` downloads the current drawn art as a PNG. Paint over it so the frames, the anchor point (feet at the bottom middle for walkers) and the size stay the same.
4. Save it under `assets/art/atlas/`, `assets/art/sheets/` or `assets/art/rigs/` with the same name. The dev server reloads and the new art shows.

Keep the paper look so hand-made pictures sit well beside the drawn ones (`docs/STYLE.md` has the full style, line weights and prompts). That means cream paper with a sticker border about 3–4 px wide around the shape (the drawn art gets it from `sticker()`/`makeSheet()`), dark ink outlines (`#2a2130`), rounded shapes and a little grain. Transparent pixels are see-through in the world, and the colors are plain sRGB (color management is off).

## What benefits most

Ranked by how much a hand-made picture would add for the work involved:

| Priority | Art | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Item icons**: one cell for each of the 253 items, starting with weapons, tools, armor, boss drops, fish and badges | `atlas/` | Shown at 64 px in the backpack, hotbar, tooltips, museum and the player's hand, all the time. One cell per item, independent of each other, so they can be done a few at a time. |
| 1 | **Trading cards**: `card_<key>` for each of the 24 cards, `cardback0`–`cardback3` for the backs | `atlas/` | Each card's face is painted at boot from the game's own sketch, so a hand-made card replaces it by name. The Full Art cards (the Great Crane, the Mainspring, the Leviathan, the Folio, The Unfolded) are made to be painted edge to edge; keep the card's outline and its rarity mark in the corner. |
| 2 | **Pets and the mount**: `pet_frog`, `pet_kit`, `pet_moth`, `pet_crease` (2 frames of 96×96), `stag` (3 frames of 160×112) | `sheets/` | These are cosmetic rewards, so they should look special. (Their backpack icons are separate atlas cells, which also come from `drawPet`/`drawStag`.) |
| 3 | **UI pictures**: badges, the tab icons (`cellIcon`), the heart, the coin | `atlas/` | Small, but always on screen. |
| 4 | **Boss rig parts**: the Great Crane, the Inkwell Leviathan, the Charred Folio, The Unfolded, The Mainspring, the King Slime | not yet | The largest characters on screen, each with a single look, so a painted piece per part would pay off. This needs a per-part override in `rigSkin` first (issue #48). |

## What stays procedural

- **Terrain blocks and walls**: seeded speckle, grain and a shared outline make tiling look hand-made already. The normal map is built from the same art, and the ores have to redraw for color vision. Painted blocks are possible (`atlas/dirt.png`), but every tile of that kind would repeat the same picture.
- **Particles, lighting, bloom and grading, the map**: these are effects, not pictures.
- **Diorama layers and foreground cutouts**: one 2048×512 strip per layer, biome and season (more than 80), drawn lazily as the camera nears. Hand-painting them would be a large project of its own.
- **Rigged characters** (the player, townsfolk, partners, every foe and every boss): each rig part is painted for a skin (`rigSkin`), so one design covers every look, armor set and color variant, which painted images can't do. Their entries in `SHEETS` (`slime`, `folio`, `p_lumi`, the townsfolk `guide`…) only feed the bestiary sketches and portraits. Replacing one changes that still picture, not the character in the world. Painted rig parts would need a per-part override in `rigSkin`, and that is only worth it for a character with a single look (a boss, a partner).
- **Status overlays** (`pstatus`) and the elite star: small effects drawn over sprites.

## Painted so far

Generated with Nano Banana Pro (Higgsfield or OpenRouter) from the prompts in `tools/art/`, then cut out and restyled by `tools/art/build.py` (it redraws the ink outline and cream border at the `docs/STYLE.md` weight).

- **In the game:** all 32 weapon, tool and shield icons (`assets/art/atlas/`: `swWood` … `swEm`, `foldblade`, `pickCu` … `pickEm`, `hammer`, `shuri`, `bowW`, `bowG`, `bowFr`, `bowMoon`, `launch`, `launchMoon`, `tomeInk`, `tomeCrane`, `tomeTide`, `tomeMoon`, `staff`, `staffEm`, `shwood`, `buckler`, `quilt`, `beacon`) and the Fold Fox (`assets/art/sheets/foldfox.png`).
- **Item icons** (`assets/art/atlas/`, sheets and prompts in `tools/art/item-sheets.json`, cut with `build.icon_cells()`): bars (`barCu` … `barSky`), materials (`gel`, `lens`, `batwing`, `fstar`, `inksac`, `moonink`, `cog`, `goldleaf`), boss drops and summons (`plume`, `inkheart`, `cinder`, `crown`, `charm`, `inkwell`, `bookmark`, `heart`), potions, tonics and food (`potion`, `mpot`, `potSwift` … `potGhost`, `sunTart`, `bread`, `grilled`, `mcrys`, `bpUp`), warhammers (`hamcu` … `hamfo`), `swFo`, `pickFo`, `swSky`, hooks, `crowbar`, `wellnib`, the metal shields (`shcu` … `shfo`, `patch`), rods and bait (`rodW` … `rodEm`, `fly`, `glowlure`, `moonlure`, `leaflure`), arrows (`arrow` … `rarrow`), `paper`, `bobber`, all 16 fish (`minnow` … `magmaw`), accessories (`kite`, `toolbelt`, `magnet`, `pendant`, `glider`, `lantern`, `ribbon`, `clockwings`), `starlens`, `archkey`, the trick tools (`ripper`, `needle`, `folder`) and the buckets, all armour (`helmcu` … `legsfo` and the three sets `helm_warden` … `legs_weave`), all 15 badges (`bStomp` … `bLure`), `coin`, `hpHeart`, `mpStar`, furniture (`torch`, `table`, `chair`, `chest`, `bench`, `furnace`, `anvil`, `bed`, `alchemy`, `shelf`, `candle`, `pot`, `clock`, `armchair`, `plant`, `fcrate`; these cells are also the placed tiles, so they are cut with `floor` to stand on the bottom of the cell), the paintings (`paint1` … `paint5`) and banners (`banR`, `banB`, `banG`), herbs, rare plants and their seeds (`herbs.0` … `rareSeeds.3`), `mush`, `soggy`, `tackle`, `tmap`, `binder`, `cardpack`, `cardpackg`, the seven fossils (`fosAmm` … `fosWing`), the pet and mount icons (`petFrog`, `petKit`, `petMoth`, `petCrease`, `stag`), `crane`, `ropeIt` and `doorIcon`. Rig parts (`assets/art/rigs/`, cut with `build.rig_parts()`, prompts in `tools/art/rig-sheets.json`): every boss and every foe is painted in the style of the painted player model (`tools/art/pending/player_parts.webp`), designed from its name, habitat and boss title card. The eight human foes (zombie, knight, sentinel, ash imp, Stack Warden, Stargazer, Scrap Foreman, Drowned Scribe) and the ten townsfolk are each a whole painted cut-out keyed to their skin (`human@zombie.root+all`), with painted animation frames as variants of it: `blink`, `w1`/`w2` (walk steps), and `wind`, `atk`, `hurt` for foes or `happy`, `surprised`, `sad`, `angry` for townsfolk (`human@guide.root+all.happy.webp`). Separate painted heads, bodies and legs read as loose stickers at that size, so they are painted whole. The `p*` clips (`pidle` breathes and blinks, `pwalk` steps, `pwind`, `pdash`, `prest`, `patk`, `pthrow`, `phurt`) swap the frames through the root's `sw` channel and add a bob and lean; `foeClip()` picks them from the foe's act (and `throwT` after a throw), townsfolk walk and idle with them, and a dialogue portrait is the expression frame cropped to the head (`headBox` in `dialogue.js`). A frame that isn't painted falls back to the standing one. Each character's frames are painted in one render so they share a scale (prompts in `tools/art/rig-sheets.json`), found with `build.blobs()` (it drops ground lines and slivers) and cut with `build.frame_set()`, which puts every frame on one canvas with the feet on the bottom edge and the hips on the centre line, so swapping frames doesn't make the figure jump. Keep the pose the same between frames that alternate (a walk frame that drops the held item or changes the face flickers): repaint, or reuse the standing frame for the second step. The player is now the painted hero (the `hero` rig in `rig.js`, CLAUDE.md "The painted hero"): `H.head` (and one painted head per expression), `H.torso`, `H.arm` and `H.leg` at 4× design size with `.mask` pictures for the tunic and skin colours, cut from renders made with the pending player model as the design reference and the townsfolk as the quality reference, left with their own thin ink (the rig draws one outline round the whole figure). The hairstyles and hats (`H.hair.<style>`, `H.hat.<style>`) are edits of a render of the hero's own bald head (`tools/art/extract.py` aligns each render to the reference and keeps what was added; paint on a green reference when the piece is light grey), the cape, scarf and satchel (`H.cape.b`/`.f`, `H.scarf.b`/`.f`, `H.pack.b`/`.f`) edits of a render of the whole hero (`tools/art/extract_body.py`), and the gripping fist (`H.arm.grip`, `H.fist`) an edit of the arm holding a green stick (`tools/art/grip.py`). The armour (`H.helm.<k>`, `H.mail.<k>`, `H.greave.<k>`, `H.brace.<k>` for each metal and set) is edits of a rest-pose render of the hero without its front arm and of the arm alone, with the body render as the second reference so the arm pieces match (`tools/art/extract_armor.py`, `extract_armarm.py`). Before that, the player couldn't be painted whole (the look is customizable and it has dozens of poses), so it wears painted layers: `L.<layer>.<part>[.<style>]` pictures in light greys (`L.body.head`, `L.hair.head.short`, `L.hat.head.cap`, `L.shirt.torso`, `L.armor.armA`...) that replace one layer's drawing on one human part, fitted to the bounds of that drawing and tinted (multiply) with the look's colour for the layer (`hlArt`, `LSTY`, `LCOL` in `rig.js`; the straw hat is painted in colour and not tinted). The face stays drawn on top, so its expressions and blink still work; the long-hair cap and the bun are still drawn. The four partners are whole painted cut-outs too (`p_lumi.root+all` … `p_ember.root+all`, one render each, cut by `tools/art/partners.py`, prompts under `partners` in `tools/art/rig-sheets.json`), with frames for their idle and blink, their walk (`w1`, `w2`) or wing beat (`flap`), their move (`flare` for Lumi's Flare and Ember's Rekindle, `snip`/`snip2`, `dig`) and `cheer`; their `p*` clips in `partners.js` (`pidle`, `pmove`, `pact`, `pcheer`) swap the frames, and the Party tab, HUD and dialogue portraits crop the painted figure (`pBox`). The walkers stand on their feet (`build.frame_set()`); the fliers have no feet to line up, so `aligned_set()` places each frame where its ink best overlaps the idle frame's. A painted frame that glows (a flare) draws its outline warm brown, which the ink-line restyle can't find: `deglow()` turns it to ink first, or `inlay()` keeps the idle frame's outline and paints the glowing frame inside it. Ember's frames share one tall canvas (wings up and down), so its root has `artS: 1.9`, which scales a merged painting about the drawn bounds. The bats, the Bookmoth and the Paper Crane have their wings painted onto the body (the crane is one whole piece), so no wing covers a face. The creatures' faces are painted too, with each blink or open mouth painted beside its open frame so the two match; a few stay drawn where the painted one came out wrong (the slime's mouth, the Grand Nib's blink, the Folio's eye white). Cutting: renders rarely keep their pieces inside grid cells, so `rig_parts(..., loose=True)` finds each row's pieces as blobs, left to right (`row_stickers`); pieces made of two parts (a pair of eyes) need the plain grid cut. To paint a rig: lay its drawn parts out in a grid from `artExport('rigs', ...)`, give the model the player style as the first reference and that layout as the second, and ask it to keep each piece's cell, facing, silhouette and width-to-height ratio. Still drawn: the block, ore and wall cells (world textures that must tile), the trading cards (painted from the sprite sheets at boot) and the projectile cells.
- **Pending, not wired in yet** (`tools/art/pending/`): painted player parts in side view with a tint mask, painted armour for the six metal sets (a head wearing the helmet, torso, arm and leg per metal), the two leafy tree canopies and a two-tile trunk with roots. They need code: per-part overrides in `rigSkin` for the player and armour, and region overrides (the canopies are 256×256 atlas regions, not cells, and the trunk is drawn wider than its cell) for the trees.
- **Prompts:** `tools/art/weapon-prompts.txt` (the icon sheet format), `tools/art/armor-prompts.txt`, `tools/art/grip-prompt.txt` (fists that grip the held item).
